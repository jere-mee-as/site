An aesthetic

Chic Modernist

Clean lines, solid colour, one accent — worn on a person or built into a room.

Tap, swipe or press → to walk through

What it is

Smart casual, cut clean.

Dressed up just enough: presentable, never fussy. It sits near high fashion and near Paris, but leans on neither.

Elevation · 01

One rule, two scales

It dresses a room the way it dresses a person.

Pale walls, steel-and-cane chairs, a long table. Pale blue tailoring. The one accent is in the shoes.

Where it came from · 1919–1933

A school that put art, craft and industry in one room.

The Bauhaus: Weimar, then Dessau, then Berlin. Shown: Joost Schmidt’s mechanical stage design, 1925–26 — primary colours, pure volumes.

Brno · 1930

Villa Tugendhat.

Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich. Its great living-room panes were built to sink, motor-driven, into the floor below.

The wall is gone. The garden is the room.

America · mid-century

Then into the office.

Hans and Florence Knoll carried the idea into American showrooms and workplaces. Florence had studied under Mies.

The look

Mostly solid. Patterns, sparingly. One accent.

II · The wardrobe · her

A plain top. A wide black trouser. One good bag.

Her list runs to long coats, turtlenecks, fitted jeans, blazers, tailored skirts and dresses, a heel, pearls.

Elevation · 02

The list

Eight pieces. Four hers, four his.

Each is one plain thing, done well. Put any two together and you are dressed.

One rule, two wardrobes

Pale on top. Dark to anchor it.

The same idea fits either of them: a light knit, a quiet trouser, one dark object to finish.

Outerwear

The coat does the work.

A trench or a long wool coat, on her or on him, and the outfit is finished.

Check. Stripe. Paisley. Tweed.

Patterns, sparingly: strike all but one. Here it is a rust tweed trouser, under a plain navy knit.

Detail

The finish is in the hardware.

A brown belt through ivory knit and navy pleats; a small pendant. Elsewhere: a watch, pearls.

III · Beyond clothes

Then the house.

A wall of glass, low pale sofas, one sculpture, red cushions as the accent — the wardrobe, at the scale of a room.

Its picture

It photographs best in black and white.

Take the colour away and nothing is lost: the look is all shape and value. A dark coat, a pale street, two people.

Less is more.

The motto Mies van der Rohe made his own. Restraint read as confidence.

Barcelona · 1929

Then leave the room almost empty.

The German Pavilion, by Mies and Reich: travertine, onyx, glass and still water. Nothing else.
