The parts list

The Freedom Tower in Miami (1925, Schultze & Weaver) copies the Giralda, the bell tower of Seville Cathedral. So does the Biltmore in Coral Gables, by the same firm, a year later.

The Breakers in Palm Beach (1926) took its twin belvederes from the Villa Medici in Rome.

The Wolfsonian on Miami Beach began in 1927 as the Washington Storage Company — a warehouse with a carved Spanish Baroque doorway.

Everglades Club · Palm Beach · 1919

Addison Mizner’s first Palm Beach building. The town has looked like this since.

Mizner and Singer

Paris Singer, heir to the sewing-machine fortune, hired the society architect Addison Mizner to build a convalescent home for officers of the First World War. The war ended first; it opened in 1919 as a private club.

Mizner went on to make his own roof tile, pottery, ironwork and furniture to supply the style, and laid out Via Mizner off Worth Avenue as a little Spanish lane.

Hotel Nacional · Havana · 1930

The style crossed the water with the winter crowd.

Havana

Designed by the New York firm McKim, Mead & White and opened in 1930 on a rise above the Malecón, the sea wall.

The same towers, tile roofs and loggias as Palm Beach, ninety miles south: the winter season ran between them by steamer, rail and seaplane.

The grammar

Half-round clay barrel tile on low roofs; thick white or ochre stucco; round arches on every opening; wrought-iron grilles, lanterns and balconies.

Above all, shade you can walk through: loggias, arcades, courtyards with a fountain, striped awnings. White walls throw the heat back.

Out of the glare: terracotta underfoot, beams overhead, plaster smoothed by hand.

Interiors

Saltillo and terracotta floors, dark-stained beams, iron stair rails, hand-painted tile on stair risers and fountains.

Florida added its own material: pecky cypress, swamp cypress pocked by fungus, used for ceilings and panelling.

The grandest version is Vizcaya on Biscayne Bay (1916), James Deering’s house, furnished with Italian and Spanish rooms bought whole.

III · Four o’clock

The tile, worn.

Cobalt on white, from the stair riser to the shirt.

Tile

Cobalt-and-white glazed tile came to Spain with Moorish potters; Seville and Valencia made it an industry. In 1920s Florida it went on stair risers, fountains, door surrounds and bathroom walls.

Today’s resortwear borrows the same two colours: big blue leaf and palm prints on white linen and cotton.

The resort wardrobe

Linen, terry, eyelet and print. Cut loose, worn with sandals.

Whites and sand from the walls; cobalt, green and yellow from the tile and the garden.

Who makes it now

Shirt dresses and camp-collar shirts in linen, terry polos, eyelet cotton, tailored swim shorts with matching shirts, espadrilles and flat sandals.

Orlebar Brown (London, 2007) built a label on tailored swim shorts; Vilebrequin (Saint-Tropez, 1971) on printed ones. Brunello Cucinelli, Ralph Lauren and Margaret Howell do the linen.

IV · Dusk  —  Hayes Mansion, San Jose

California

Hayes Mansion (1905, George Page) is an early West Coast version. In California the style met real Spanish colonial roots: the missions.

Bertram Goodhue’s 1915 exposition in San Diego set the fashion; Pasadena’s City Hall (1927) followed, and Paul R. Williams built Mediterranean houses across Los Angeles.

Nobody in Seville built this.

It is the Mediterranean as remembered by people who went there in winter: a romance, not a copy. The 2020s revival keeps the dream — rattan, terrazzo, white walls, cobalt and citrus.

How it ended, and came back

The Florida boom broke in 1926: land prices collapsed, and a hurricane hit Miami that September. The Depression finished the building spree.

The houses stayed. Preservation districts in Palm Beach, Coral Gables and Miami Beach protect them, and interiors since about 2020 have borrowed the look again.

Rules

  1. White walls, clay roofs.
  2. Every opening is an arch.
  3. Build the shade: arcade, loggia, courtyard.
  4. Ornament is tile and iron.
  5. Dress in linen the colour of the walls.
