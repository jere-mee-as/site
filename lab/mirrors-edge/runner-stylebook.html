01Reading the reference

Seven things the city does

From the 29 images in your stylebook folder. Numbers in red are the file numbers they come from. Everything after this section follows from these seven.

REF 04 · 08 · 22 · 25

White is overexposed

Surfaces blow out to white the way a camera does at noon. Bloom eats the edges of the frame. The page is never grey; it is lit.

REF 04 · 11 · 29

Shadows are blue

The shade side of a white building is sky blue, not grey. Every shadow, divider and secondary surface in the UI takes that blue.

REF 01 · 11 · 13 · 24

Red means go here

Runner Vision. Pipes, ramps and doors turn red when they are the way forward, and go back to white once used. Red is never decoration.

REF 06 · 07 · 12 · 14 · 15 · 18 · 20

One color per place

An orange corridor, a yellow catwalk, a green office, an ultramarine server hall. Each interior owns one saturated hue against white.

REF 13 · 14 · 16 · 20

Numbers are architecture

Room and floor numbers are painted huge and condensed straight onto walls: 05, 801, 1000, 110. Wayfinding as the main graphic.

REF 25

UI is a frosted slab

A tall translucent color panel down one side, blurring the world behind it. Thin wide caps, a white tab, one hairline rule, outlined buttons.

REF 21 · 22 · 23 · 26 · 27

Flat painted shapes

The cutscenes and paintings strip the city to flat planes: navy, cornflower, white, and one red or orange accent. Hard edges, no texture.

05Navigation

The route is the menu

There is no nav bar, no list of links at the top, no hamburger. You stand on a roof. Something is red. You run to it. Each report is a rooftop in the city with its number painted on the wall, and the route links them in order, newest first.

Top-down: the map overlay draws the city like this, in blues (ref 29)

Run

Click the red object, or press Space / → / Enter. The camera runs, jumps the gap and lands on the next roof. Swipe left on a phone.

Quick turn

The 180° turn from the game is the back button. Q, ←, Backspace, or swipe right. The browser's own Back does the same through the URL hash.

Map

M or tap the clock. The camera lifts to a top-down blue city with every drop numbered. Click a number to run straight there.

Deliver

On a package roof the frosted slab slides in. Deliver opens the report. Every report is still a real link underneath for keyboards and screen readers.

Time trial

The clock runs from your first step. Each roof is a checkpoint with a split. Reach the end to see your run and your best.

The board

The drop roof carries the manifest of all eight packages. Picking one lights a red line across the city to it and starts the run.

06Motion & sound

Momentum, then stillness

Run

Accelerate over 300ms, field of view widens from 70° to 84°, head bob at stride rate, speed streaks gather at the edges.

Jump

An arc over the gap, a beat of floating at the top, then a landing dip of 0.4m and a short roll. The used red object fades to white.

Arrive

Everything stops. The slab cuts in from the left in 2 frames. The wall number is already in view. Stillness is the reward.

Sound

Synthesized, no files. Footfalls on concrete, wind rising with speed, a landing thud, a soft UI tick. On by default after the first key, S mutes.

Reduced motion

No run. A white bloom cut to the next roof, same arrival.

Idle

The camera breathes. Clouds drift. Nothing else moves until you do.

08Guardrails

Ours, not theirs

  • No game logo, no DICE or EA marks, no character. The runner is the camera; there's no figure on screen.
  • No screenshots or game audio in the site. The city is generated in code, the sounds are synthesized.
  • In-world brands are invented: halden logistics, vesta 9, kuro & assoc., meridian. The style is borrowed, the names aren't.
  • The site keeps <ol id="list"><!-- LIST --></ol> so build.py drops reports in unchanged.
