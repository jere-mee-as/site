the city web

What an ordinary website looks like in a city that is clean, quiet and watched. Who builds them, what they're legally required to show you, how they move, what they sound like. Then one brief: the website for a residential tower called vesta 9.

01The premise

Every site is a lobby

The City has no ugly websites. Building permits and web licenses come from the same office, and both require the same thing: calm. A site is treated like the ground floor of a tower. White marble, one plant, a receptionist who smiles and writes your name down.

Underneath, every page is monitored. The law says sites must tell you so, and they do, very nicely. Runners exist because nothing typed into a City website is private.

"Your comfort is our infrastructure."
Standard footer line, Central District
02Design law

Seven rules every City site follows

1
White means trust. Pages are white or the palest blue. Dark sites read as hiding something and don't get licensed for residential use.
2
One brand color. Never red. Red belongs to emergency services by statute. In practice red belongs to runners, which is why a red mark on a City page means someone got in.
3
Lowercase is calm. Brand names are lowercase and hairline thin: vesta 9, halden logistics, meridian. Capitals are for labels and legal text only.
4
Motion is slow and level. Drone shots, gentle reveals, an elevator that glides. Nothing shakes, flashes or bounces, unless something is wrong.
5
Numbers prove safety. Days without incident, air quality, compliance percentage. Every site carries at least one counting-up number.
6
The notice never leaves. A monitoring line runs across the top of every page, and a consent bar meets you at the bottom. You can accept. You can accept all.
7
Two scripts. English first, Japanese beneath, in labels and signage. The City is bilingual on its walls, so its sites are too.

05Motion

The elevator

Drone hero

A slow orbit around the tower in the same white city as the run. One revolution every four minutes.

Scrolling is an elevator

A floor indicator on the right counts 01 to 96 as you go down the page. Each section is a floor. A chime plays when you arrive.

Reveals

Blocks rise 18px and settle in 420ms on a soft curve. Headlines unmask upward. Numbers count up once.

Ticker

The civic line drifts left forever at reading speed.

Cameras

Live feed tiles with grain and a clock. Mostly CLEAR. Sometimes MOTION · ROOF EAST for a second, then CLEAR again.

When it breaks

The intrusion is the only sharp motion on the site: a 2-frame jolt, a red mark, then stillness.

06Voice

Euphemism, gently

Second person, short, warm. Nothing is ever a problem; it's an incident. People aren't watched; they're looked after. The roof isn't closed; access is limited to licensed staff. Runners are never named. They're unlicensed rooftop activity.

Says

Peace of mind, by design. · Your file has been forwarded. · We noticed unusual activity. · Quiet is a feature.

Never says

Surveillance. Police. Free. Exciting. Anything with an exclamation mark.

10Revision 2

The thin paper

After an audit, the first build still followed today's web: a long scroll, pill buttons, cards, glass blur, fade-ups, a chat bubble, corner toasts. Revision 2 keeps it a website and drops those habits.

Fixed screens

No scrolling. Six floors, each one screen. A tall slab menu in the floor's color; hard cuts with a short tone between floors. Long content pages inside the frame.

The gate

Entry replaces the cookie bar. Enter, or Enter and accept all. Every preference reads Required.

Surveillance as structure

Thin brackets lock onto whatever you rest on and time your dwell. The comfort record writes down every move. Your interests come back to you in announcements.

The thin paper

Pure white surface, and it stays that way. Press on a pale floor number and the paper gives: the cursor backlights it, showing resident files, camera cones over the floor plans, the perimeter, your own name. Press again to let it settle. Only the runner's note on the roof tells you how.

White-out

Some lines are printed white on white. Select them to read. The site notices, and after the second time it asks you to stop.

The roof

A locked floor in the menu. When a runner gets in it turns red and opens: wind, the dossier showing through, a note. When you come back down, the building has noticed.

Also: announcements as a full-width band with a chime and an optional spoken voice; the concierge as an intercom on the lobby; the application as a five-step kiosk with a floor keypad; idle mode after a minute; room tone per floor.

11Guardrails

Fiction, built honestly

vesta 9, CivicSafe, halden logistics and the rest are invented. No real company, logo or game asset. The forms ask for nothing sensitive and send nothing anywhere: the application takes a name, household size, floor and month, and the Quiet List takes an email it never stores. The footer says it's fiction.
